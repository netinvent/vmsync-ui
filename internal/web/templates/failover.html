Roll back…
This replaces {{.VM}}'s disks and cannot be undone from here. Its current contents are kept on the target as <disk>.vmsync-replaced-<time> files, which nothing removes for you.
A rollback is for promoting, not for carrying on replicating: the next sync from {{if .PeerRef}}{{.PeerRef}}{{else}}the source{{end}} would put back exactly what you rolled away from. So replication into {{.VM}} is left paused and its schedule is switched off. To go back to replicating afterwards, set its role to target and then run a full resync from the source — which rebuilds it and discards what you restored.

Full resync…
Copies {{.VM}} to {{if .PeerRef}}{{.PeerRef}}{{else}}its target{{end}} from scratch, discarding whatever the replica currently holds, and re-enables its schedule when it succeeds. This is how you go back to replicating after a rollback — set the replica's role to target first, or it will refuse to accept the copy.

Force clean resync…
Use this only when a full resync will not go through. It does everything a full resync does, and additionally undefines the target domain on {{if .PeerRef}}{{.PeerRef}}{{else}}the far end{{end}} and ignores the interlocks that protect a promoted or paused replica from being overwritten. Anything the replica holds that the source does not — a promotion's worth of writes, its restore points — is gone. It will still refuse a replica that is running: shut it down first.
